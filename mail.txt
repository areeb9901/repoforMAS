Hello everyone,

I’m Areeb, from Manipal University Jaipur, 2025 batch.

My journey with BNP Paribas actually started exactly where many of you are standing right now — with the hackathon.

That hackathon led to a six-month internship, and that internship eventually converted into the full-time role that I’m currently in.

So when I look at all of you today, I can very easily relate to what you might be feeling right now.

Some of you are probably excited.

Some of you are probably a little nervous.

And some of you are probably thinking:

“Okay… but what exactly is going to happen for 24 hours?”

And trust me, I had the same questions.

The good news is — you are going to have a blast.

Yes, it is going to be intense.

Yes, there will probably be a point somewhere in the night when your code stops making sense.

And yes, at some point, coffee might start feeling less like a beverage and more like a project dependency.

But that is also what makes the whole experience memorable.

For me, the hackathon was much more than just building a solution in 24 hours.

It was one of the first times where everything we had learned in college suddenly had to come together.

You have to understand a problem quickly, discuss ideas with your team, decide what is actually feasible, divide the work, build something, integrate everything, fix whatever inevitably breaks, and then finally present it.

And that entire experience teaches you a lot — not just technically, but also about teamwork, communication, decision-making, and working under pressure.

Since I have been on the participant side myself, I also want to share a few practical things that I genuinely wish participants think about before coming.

The first one is very simple:

**Please come rested.**

If you are travelling from far away, especially if your commute to Bengaluru is going to take three hours or more, I would genuinely recommend coming a day earlier if that is possible for you.

Because although we casually call it a “24-hour hackathon”, your actual day is much longer than that.

You have registration, settling in, the hackathon itself, final preparation and then presentations.

So realistically, you could easily be active for more than 30 hours.

And you definitely don’t want your biggest technical challenge at Hour 20 to be keeping your eyes open.

So please get some proper sleep beforehand.

Second — and this is a very important one —

you do not need to arrive after having a huge meal as if you are preparing for hibernation.

The company takes very good care of you.

Food, snacks, caffeine — all of that is properly arranged.

So your survival requirements are covered.

You just need to focus on surviving your own bugs.

Another thing I would strongly recommend:

**please make sure your laptop is working properly.**

Check your charger.

Check your development environment.

Make sure your software is installed.

Make sure you can run the things you normally work with.

Because for those 24 hours, your laptop is pretty much going to be your best friend.

And if it decides to update itself in the middle of the hackathon…

that friendship can become complicated very quickly.

Now, I know one common question everyone has before a hackathon is:

**“What skills should I prepare?”**

And my answer would be — don’t overcomplicate it.

Whatever you have learned during your four years of college, this is your chance to actually put it into practice.

If you know web development, use it.

If you have worked with machine learning algorithms, use them where they make sense.

If someone in your team is strong in frontend, someone is comfortable with backend, someone understands data or ML — combine those strengths.

The hackathon is not necessarily about knowing every technology.

It is about knowing how to use what you already know to solve a problem.

If there is one thing you want to learn quickly before coming, I would recommend understanding the basics of **deploying an application**.

Because sometimes teams build something really good, but the final few hours become stressful because they suddenly realise they also need to show it working properly.

Even a basic understanding of deployment can make your life much easier.

And one final piece of advice from my side:

**don’t try to build everything.**

A simple idea that works properly and solves the problem is far better than an extremely ambitious idea where only 40% of it works by presentation time.

Prioritise.

Build your core solution first.

Then, if you have time, add the fancy things.

And throughout the hackathon, keep talking to your teammates.

Because one of the biggest mistakes you can make is four people working brilliantly for ten hours…

only to realise at the end that none of their components work together.

Most importantly, don’t put too much pressure on yourself.

Of course, everyone wants to perform well, and that is completely natural.

But don't spend those 24 hours only thinking about the outcome.

Enjoy the experience.

Talk to people.

Experiment.

Build something you’re excited about.

Make mistakes.

Fix them.

And learn as much as you can.

Because I can tell you from my own experience — you never really know where these 24 hours might lead.

For me, they led to an internship, and eventually to the full-time role I’m in today.

So come with curiosity, come with energy, and come ready to innovate.

Don’t stress too much.

Just give it your best shot.

And I can assure you —

**you’re going to have a blast.**

All the very best to each one of you, and I hope to see some amazing ideas coming out of this edition of the hackathon.

Thank you, everyone.
